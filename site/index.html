Why it exists

One day of agent coding on a 16 GB Mac.

13Claude Code sessions open, 5 of them untouched for over 3 hours
26 hage of the oldest session, still holding its memory
5dev servers nobody remembered, on 6 ports, the oldest running for 32 hours
14.5 GBof swap in use, out of 15.4 GB

Measured on the author's Mac before TokenPet existed. It is not one machine: claude-code issue #33947 reports 107 orphaned node processes and about 7.75 GB of RAM after a normal workday, and #1935 has been open since June 2025.

The Mac window · ⌃⌥M

Find what is holding your RAM, and take it back.

Every agent session with its whole process tree (MCP servers, language servers, test runners), and the dev servers an agent started and left behind.

  • Sorted by what to do. Worth closing, your call, running so leave alone.
  • The evidence on every row. Project path, terminal, pid, asleep since when, ports, memory.
  • Nothing closes without a click. Each action names the pids first. A closed session comes back with claude --resume.

Quota

Know your limits before they stop you.

For Claude Code and Codex: how much of the 5-hour and weekly limit is used, and the exact time each resets.

  • One line, about 45 minutes ahead. Enough time to finish the task and commit.
  • The pet's face is the state. Fine, working, watch out, almost out, out, resting.
  • Facts in the panel. Forecasts only decide when to warn you.

Defaults

Quiet until it matters.

A monitor should not need monitoring. Out of the box it adds one thing to your screen and makes no sound.

  • Does not pop open by accident. A pointer just passing over the notch does not open it, and it never opens mid-drag.
  • Nothing moves while collapsed. Native Swift, about 40 MB of RAM, near 0% CPU at idle.
  • Memory warnings at most once an hour, and only when there is something you can close.

Questions

Before you install.

My Mac has no notch.

TokenPet shows as a small tab on the edge of the screen instead, by itself. You can switch to a menu bar item in Settings.

Will it close my sessions on its own?

No. Auto-close is off by default; if you turn it on, a 5-minute warning with a Keep button comes first.

Why does macOS ask for Keychain access?

Only if you choose to ask the servers for your quota. TokenPet reads the login Claude Code saved, to ask Anthropic about your own usage. Choose Always Allow to be asked once.

I pay per token through the API.

Then the quota half is not for you: TokenPet tracks subscription limits (Claude Pro and Max, ChatGPT Plus and Pro), not bills. The memory half works either way.

Which languages?

English and Vietnamese. It follows your Mac's language; you can pin one in Settings.
